### NUMBER 1 — ₹5,000-crore coastal package

Number one. MLC and State Congress working president Manjunath Bhandary said he has sought a ₹5,000-crore special economic package for coastal Karnataka to be included in the September 18 Mangaluru Cabinet meeting agenda.

He said Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar responded positively and asked the Chief Secretary to include prominent demands.

The memorandum also covers fisheries — including upgrading Mangaluru Fisheries College as a fisheries university — coastal erosion, tourism, Pilikula's urban ecopark, raising compensation for fishermen's families who die while fishing from ₹5 lakh to ₹10 lakh, and a coastal parallel road from Talapady to Byndoor.

**Host take**

If you live on the coast, this is the kind of package list people watch closely because the Cabinet meeting is in Mangaluru on September 18 — the date to remember.

### NUMBER 2 — Udupi locked overbridge

Number two. A foot overbridge near Udupi City Municipal Council — built for patients moving between the Urban Primary Health Centre and the old Mother and Child Hospital — stands locked and unused.

Social activist Nityananda Olakadu and MLA Yashpal Suvarna have urged repair, redesign or relocation. The bridge has rust, weeds and blocked staircases after the hospital site changed.

Commissioner Mahantesh Hangargi said authorities may shift it, as a new overbridge near MGM College is set to begin construction next week under a PPP model.

**Host take**

This is a simple daily-life story: a bridge built for patients that people cannot use — and officials say a replacement project starts next week.

---

### NUMBER 3 — Vande Bharat morning departure

Number three. Patrons and associations in coastal Karnataka hope a South Western Railway runtime trial of the Vande Bharat Express between Bengaluru and Mangaluru — leaving Bengaluru in the morning and returning at night — will not become the regular schedule.

They want the train to start from Mangaluru early and return by night for people with official and business work in Bengaluru. G. Hanumanth Kamath of the Pashchima Karavali Railway Yatri Abhivriddhi Samithi and KCCI president P.B. Ahmed Mudassar said a morning departure around 6 a.m. would mirror the popular Mangaluru–Thiruvananthapuram Vande Bharat pattern.

Thursday's trial covered Yeshwantpur to Mangaluru Junction in 7 hours and 5 minutes, and the return in 6 hours and 49 minutes.

**Host take**

If you commute between Mangaluru and Bengaluru, the departure time is not a small detail — it decides whether you can do a same-day round trip for work.

### NUMBER 4 — College drug screening follow-up

Number four. A follow-up drug screening on Thursday found 38 of 50 college students who had initially tested positive are now negative after counselling.

That is out of 8,310 students tested from 150 colleges this year, where 98 had first tested positive. Authorities said more than 75 percent of re-tested students reported stopping drug use, and advised continued monitoring for the remaining 12.

**Host take**

For parents and students, the scale matters here — 150 colleges screened, and authorities are tracking who clears after counselling.

### NUMBER 5 — University College poll dispute

Number five. NSUI alleged that Students' Union elections at University College, Mangalore on Friday violated state government guidelines after ABVP-backed candidates won all 37 posts.

NSUI said it learnt only on Tuesday that polls would be held Friday, despite approaching the vice-chancellor, district minister and principal to follow government orders. NSUI Dakshina Kannada convener Suhan Alva claimed the higher education secretary said elections held without following the government order would not be valid.

NSUI said it would condemn the election and demand it be declared invalid. ABVP's Suvith Shetty said the poll was peaceful.

**Host take**

Campus politics stories turn local when a union election is challenged on procedure — here the fight is over whether Friday's poll followed the state order.
