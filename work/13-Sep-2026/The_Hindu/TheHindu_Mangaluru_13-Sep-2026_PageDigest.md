### BRICS countries adopt New Delhi Declaration

India forged a consensus among BRICS countries on Saturday after Iran and the United Arab Emirates agreed to a last-minute compromise on language about the war in West Asia, allowing the 11-member grouping to adopt the New Delhi Declaration at a meeting chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The statement did not directly mention attacks against Iran by the United States and Israel or Iran's subsequent strikes on Persian Gulf neighbours including the UAE, and called for "maximum restraint" in the war while making no separate mention of the Russian war in Ukraine. The grouping declared strong criticism of Israel for its bombardment of Gaza, expressed support for South Africa's case against Israel in international courts, and called for humanitarian aid in Gaza.

### Saudi Arabia closes key oil pipeline after strikes

Threatening to send energy prices even higher amid the war in West Asia, Saudi Arabia has shut down its 1,200-kilometre-long East-West pipeline after the vital oil conduit came under aerial attack, with the kingdom saying the attack originated in Iraq where Iran-backed militias operate. The pipeline, used by Riyadh as an alternative to the Strait of Hormuz, has been moving close to 5 million barrels of oil per day in recent months, amounting to 5% of global supply. The Saudi Arabian Foreign Ministry said the attack resulted in some injuries and damage that was being assessed; though an umbrella group of Iran-backed militias in Iraq denied involvement, Iraq's government said the attacks originated from its territory, pledged a probe, and dismissed two senior officials, AP reported.

### Tulu Wikipedia marks 10 years, now has about 4,000 articles

Tulu Wikipedia, promoted by the Karavali Wikimedians User Group, celebrated its 10th anniversary with activities in Mangaluru on Saturday. ### Sugar price spike revives farmers' demand for jaggery-based ethanol units

A surge in sugar prices ahead of the festive season — Gowri-Ganesha, Dasara and Deepavali — amid reports of sugarcane diversion for ethanol has revived Karnataka farmers' demand that jaggery and grain-based units be allowed to produce ethanol. State Sugarcane Growers' Association president Kurubur Shanthakumar said ethanol procurement at around ₹65 a litre could substantially raise growers' incomes, noting Karnataka produces about 600 lakh tonnes of sugarcane annually but only 30 to 40 lakh tonnes go to some 15,000 jaggery units. Speakers at a recent Bengaluru seminar cited maize prices crossing ₹3,200 a quintal from below ₹2,800 before ethanol expansion, and said Karnataka's 56 ethanol units rank third nationally with about 135 crore litres produced annually.
