### JNCASR research finds unusual wave-like heat transport in crystalline solids

Researchers at the Jawaharlal Nehru Centre for Advanced Scientific Research (JNCASR), Bengaluru, have found an unusual way in which heat moves through the copper-based crystalline material TlCu5Se3, a finding published in Science Advances that could help develop more efficient thermoelectric technologies to convert waste heat into electricity. The material's lattice thermal conductivity was measured at about 0.3 to 0.2 watts per metre-kelvin between 294 and 673 K, with a thermoelectric figure of merit of about 1.42 at 673 K, increased to about 1.7 by introducing copper vacancies; Professor Kanishka Biswas said such ultra-low thermal conductors could aid mid-temperature energy conversion and serve as shields for quantum technologies.

### Restored films, limited reach

The Karnataka Chalanachitra Academy recently signed an MoU with NFAI for digitising and restoring 23 of its 68 analogue films, while regional efforts face steep challenges—Manipuri filmmaker Sunzu Bachaspatimayum said digitising 'Brojendragee Luhongba' (1973) took about one year and cost Rs 7 lakh, Assam has digitised only about 300 of more than 1,000 movies produced since 1935, and Kerala's Centre for International Film Research and Archives has archived six films so far. Archivists note digitisation can generate 12–18 TB per feature film, a 4K restored DCP can be 300–400 GB, and despite NFHM allocating Rs 544.82 crore between 2021-22 and 2024-25 with 1,325 films awaiting digital preservation, many restored films remain inaccessible to the average Indian viewer due to limited projection infrastructure and distribution platforms.
