## Top 5 — Mangaluru

### 1. R. Chethan posted as Western Range DIG, Amit Singh transferred

The government transferred 16 IPS officers on Saturday, posting 2010-batch officer R. Chethan as Deputy Inspector General of Police, Western Range, Mangaluru, while incumbent IGP Amit Singh moved to the Karnataka State Reserve Police. The government also downgraded the Western Range DIG post to match cadre status, and Mangaluru DCP (Law and Order) H.N. Mithun was posted as SP, Gadag.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5; News Trail Mangaluru p.3; Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.9_

### 2. Get Plastic Park ready by October-end, MP tells KIADB officials

Dakshina Kannada MP Capt. Brijesh Chowta asked KIADB officials at a DISHA meeting on Saturday to complete the Plastic Park at Ganjimutt for inauguration by October-end. Of 48 sites developed on 40 acres, 16 applications have been received, ongoing works are nearing completion, and Chowta also pressed for an action plan to saturate Koraga housing under PM-JANMAN and asked Southern Railway to explore a station site near Mangaluru International Airport.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5; Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.5_

### 3. Jumbo menace: Govt yet to release Rs 73 lakh crop loss relief for DK

At a DISHA meeting chaired by Dakshina Kannada MP Capt Brijesh Chowta in Mangaluru on Saturday, Sullia MLA Bhagirathi Murulya urged permanent measures against elephant menace, noting Rs 3.30 lakh compensation was released to 19 people in 2026-27 but Rs 73 lakh is yet to be released to 381 people whose crops were damaged by around 80 elephants in Sullia and Belthangady. Officials said an Elephant Task Force of 25 personnel is operational and farmers get a 50% subsidy for solar fencing.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.5_

### 4. MCC to launch 'Walk with Commissioner'

Mangaluru City Corporation Commissioner Rahul Ratnam Pandey will launch a monthly 'Walk with Commissioner' programme across all 60 city wards on the 16th of every month. The initiative is meant to let him inspect local problems firsthand and work toward prompt solutions for residents, with the first walk scheduled for September 16 in Ward No. 41 (Central Market).

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5_

### 5. More measures needed to make Mangaluru railway region commuter-friendly, says MLC

Congress MLC Ivan D'Souza welcomed the Railways' decision to bring the Mangaluru region under the Mysuru Division of South Western Railway from October 1 and called for more commuter-friendly measures. He criticised BJP elected representatives for remaining silent on the move and said he was ready to join a delegation led by Dakshina Kannada MP Capt. Brijesh Chowta to press Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar for improved rail connectivity.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6_

## Top 5 — Coastal Karnataka

### 1. Dharmasthala burial case: SIT urges court to take cognisance of final report

The SIT probing the Dharmasthala mass burial case asked the Belthangady court on Saturday to take cognisance of its final report on a false complaint filed by C.N. Chinna and sought action against him and five activists. Prosecutor Divyaraj Hegde said the 7,005-page report accuses Chinna and others of multiple offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for fabricating evidence related to alleged mass burials, and the court posted the matter to October 3.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5_

### 2. Bikarnakatte-Sanur project 76.1% complete, says NHAI official

The four-laning of the 45.01-km Bikarnakatte (Mangaluru)–Sanur (Karkala) stretch of NH 169 has reached 76.1% completion, with 36.42 km ready, NHAI Mangaluru official Abdulla Javed Azmi told a DISHA meeting on Saturday. The ₹1,137-crore project is now expected to finish by March 2027, though a 1-km stretch near Rosa Mistica School at Gurupura-Kaikamba remains stalled under a court stay, and a six-lane bridge on NH 66 across the Phalguni at Kulur is 58% complete.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5_

### 3. Illegal trekking, 'hidden spot' tourism raise alarm in Western Ghats

Residents and environmentalists in Kodagu and Chikkamagaluru have raised alarm over private groups and tour operators promoting remote natural spots on social media for commercial trekking without Forest Department permission. Locals warn increased footfall is leaving plastic waste and threatening ecologically fragile forests and wildlife habitats, and Forest Minister Ramalinga Reddy said he has directed officials to act against illegal commercial trekking after complaints from residents.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.7_

### 4. Large wasp nest removed

A large wasp nest spotted on a roadside tree in front of the KSRTC depot at Mukrampady in Puttur was removed on Friday night following complaints from residents who feared monkeys disturbing the nest could trigger an attack. Puttur MLA Ashok Rai directed party workers to take immediate steps, and the nest was destroyed on Friday evening using a crane and fire, with more than 50 party workers and locals participating.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.5_

### 5. Karwar med college to be named after Hegde: CM

Chief Minister D K Shivakumar announced that the government would name the Karwar medical college and a major road in Bengaluru's Shivaram Karanth Layout after Ramakrishna Hegde, and institute the 'Ramakrishna Hegde best panchayat award' to be presented annually. Shivakumar said Hegde built leaders, transcended caste in politics, and his panchayat raj experiments became a model that inspired the 73rd and 74th constitutional amendments.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.7; The Hindu Mangaluru p.7_

## Top 5 — Karnataka

### 1. KPSC 'scam': CID detains IAS officer Gangwar

The Criminal Investigation Department (CID) on Saturday detained IAS officer Gyanendra Kumar Gangwar in connection with alleged irregularities in the Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC) recruitment examination for veterinary officers, after questioning him for more than nine hours on Friday. CID sources said Gangwar was taken into custody for allegedly not cooperating with the investigation and giving contradictory statements, focusing on his role as Controller of Examinations at KPSC from February 2024 to March 2026, while the CID and ED continue separate probes into alleged manipulation of examination records and OMR sheets.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.3; The Hindu Mangaluru p.7_

### 2. About 50 from State return home safe; 24 still missing in Nepal floods

About 50 people from Karnataka who were stranded after flash floods in Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region returned to Bengaluru on Saturday, while 24 from the State remain missing according to the State Emergency Operations Centre. Four people previously presumed missing are now in contact with authorities and can be reached at 1070 or 080-22253707; families of a Whitefield pilgrimage group of 67 and a group of 10 US and Canadian citizens originally from Bengaluru and Udupi at Rasuwagadhi still await word from officials.

_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.3; Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.7; Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.7_

### 3. 1,000 acres of forest in Ballari turned into revenue land

More than 1,000 acres of scrub forest in Ballari's Sandur, protected as reserved forest for more than a century, has been turned into revenue land through an order passed by revenue authorities without keeping the forest department informed. The 1,096-acre parcel in Somalapur village was notified as forest by the Madras Presidency on April 1, 1890 but was converted to 'B-kharab' non-forest revenue land via an illegal mutation on May 30, 2024, without clearance required under the Forest (Conservation) Act 1980; activist Dinesh Kallahalli obtained RTI documents and called for a CBI probe.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.3; Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.9_

### 4. Grand Dasara, little reward for its folk artists

For thousands of folk artists, performing in the Mysuru Dasara Jamboo Savari is a matter of pride, but many complain of low remuneration and inadequate facilities. Around 1,500 folk artists from across Karnataka present more than 60 folk art forms on Vijayadashami, performing non-stop for nearly four hours, yet last year Jamboo Savari participants received Rs 875 per person per day for two days—far below the Department of Kannada and Culture's 2016 guideline of Rs 20,000 a day for a team of at least eight artists, while the government spent Rs 43 crore on Dasara in 2025.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.1_

### 5. LOST IN TRANSITION? 2 months on, hit by teething trouble, VB-G RAM G lacks bite

The first two months of the Viksit Bharat-Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) (VB-G RAM G) have faced administrative, technical and financial challenges in Karnataka, with grassroots officials struggling to adapt to a system that replaced the two-decades-old MGNREGA. Karnataka started implementing the scheme on July 23; 805 of 5,958 gram panchayats reported no expenditure and only 1.69 lakh households received work in July and 3.51 lakh in August, while RDPR Minister Eshwar Khandre said the government would allocate sufficient funds and target 1.5 crore person-days a month.

_Sources: Deccan Herald DH on Sunday p.7_
