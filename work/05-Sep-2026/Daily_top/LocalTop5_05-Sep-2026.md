## Top 5 — Mangaluru

### 1. Two NIT-K professors held over fake bills
Police arrested two NIT-K Surathkal associate NCC officers — P. Sam Johnson of the Department of Mathematical and Computational Sciences and H. Shivananda Nayaka of the Department of Mechanical Engineering — for claiming ₹13.51 lakh in travel, accommodation and daily allowance using forged NCC camp documents; director B. Ravi's complaint alleged Johnson swindled ₹12.32 lakh and Nayaka ₹1.18 lakh for duties claimed between May 5, 2023 and May 16, 2026, including ₹1.18 lakh each for a Pre-Thal Sainik Camp-II in Bengaluru from June 28 to July 7, 2025 using the same fake detailment order.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.3; News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

### 2. Worm allegedly found inside egg: Blinkit facility sealed
Food safety officials inspected a Blinkit online retail facility in Mangaluru after a customer alleged that a worm was found inside an egg purchased through the quick-commerce platform, with the complaint reportedly submitted to Health Minister U T Khader. During inspection of the facility at Falnir Square in Falnir, officials found packets containing broken eggs and directed staff to remove and dispose of affected stock; the storage room was temporarily closed and sealed for cleaning and sanitisation, and the Food Safety Department said it would issue a notice seeking an explanation. In a separate action in Bengaluru, the Department of Food Safety and Drug Administration closed and seized a kitchen at Chairman's Club in Sahakarnagar's Dollars Colony after finding around 40 kg of expired chilli and alleged violations including improper labelling, unhygienic conditions and storage of vegetarian and non-vegetarian food together.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3; Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.2_

### 3. 16-coach Vande Bharat to undergo Yeshwanthpur–Mangaluru test run
A 16-coach Vande Bharat Express will undertake a trial run between Yeshwanthpur and Mangaluru Central on Saturday, September 5, as directed by the Railway Board. The train will leave Yeshwanthpur at 6.05am and arrive at Mangaluru Central at 2.25pm, halting at Hassan, Sakleshpur, Subrahmanya Road and Padil; it returns from Mangaluru Central at 3pm and reaches Yeshwanthpur at 11pm. The trial assumes importance as the route includes the challenging ghat section, where officials will assess operational performance under varying gradients and explore reducing the proposed journey time of more than eight hours without compromising safety.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

The Mangaluru City Police on Friday issued a traffic advisory for Mosaru Kudike programmes across the city from Saturday evening on Sri Krishna Janmashtami, urging motorists to avoid congested routes. Mathias Road-Highland Junction), and Kulur Ferry Road near Kottara (via KSRTC-Kuntikana).
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.4_

The accused — Rithil Poojary, Prem Bhandari and Sanjay Hegde — allegedly entered the cafe near the Moodbidri bus stand without permission and questioned the students; two of the accused are alleged to be Sangh Parivar activists. ## Top 5 — Coastal Karnataka

### 1. No end to cyber fraud: ₹26 crore lost in two and a half years
Despite sustained awareness campaigns, cyber fraud continues to pose a serious threat in Uttara Kannada, with fraudsters allegedly siphoning off nearly ₹26 crore from residents over the past two and a half years. According to the state Home Department, 237 cyber fraud cases were registered at the Uttara Kannada cyber police station during the period, 14 accused were arrested, and only around ₹1.85 crore could be recovered; fraudsters have used fake online job offers, stock market investment scams, impersonation of bank or government officials, KYC update requests and social media targeting. The Uttara Kannada police have announced a video competition on cyber safety, with submissions due to the district police social media cell by September 20.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

### 2. 'Allow outstation voters' kin to respond to SIR notices'
Majlis-e-Islah-wa-Tanzeem, a century-old social organisation in Bhatkal, has urged Karnataka authorities to allow adult family members to respond to Special Intensive Revision (SIR) notices and submit required documents on behalf of voters residing abroad or outside the state. General Secretary Azizur Rahman Ruknuddin Nadvi met Uttara Kannada Deputy Commissioner K. Lakshmi Priya in Karwar and submitted a memorandum, citing a large number of Bhatkal voters living abroad or outside Karnataka and a Telangana Chief Electoral Officer directive of August 27, 2026 permitting adult family members aged above 18 to appear before EROs or AEROs on behalf of senior citizens, persons with disabilities, those who are ill and overseas voters.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

## Top 5 — Karnataka

### 1. Tunnel may damage Lalbagh rock, warns GSI
A Geological Survey of India expert committee has flagged serious geological, structural and groundwater risks to Lalbagh and its three-billion-year-old Lalbagh rock in a 27-page report submitted to the government in April. The committee warned that tunneling and excavation could damage the ancient rock mass, widen existing cracks and threaten the Kempegowda tower, and flagged proposed Shaft 4 adjacent to the National Geological Monument as a critical concern. The findings are significant as the state government was on the verge of awarding the Rs 22,267-crore, 16.75-km Hebbal–Central Silk Board tunnel road project to Adani Enterprises, the lowest bidder.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.1_

### 2. Drought-hit Karnataka launches cloud seeding operation
The Karnataka government launched its Rs 28.52 crore cloud seeding programme at HAL airport in Bengaluru on Friday, with Water Resources Minister N Chaluvarayaswamy flagging off a special light aircraft to spray chemicals including silver iodide and potassium iodide into dense clouds to induce rain in drought-hit parts of the state. The minister said an earlier attempt was 35% successful and the government expects rain within 30 to 60 minutes of seeding.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.4_

### 3. Govt vehicles violate traffic rules, pile up over 82k cases in 3 years
Karnataka government vehicles, including police and non-police department fleets, accumulated over 82,000 traffic violation cases between 2023 and July 2026 with Rs 19.56 crore in unpaid penalties, data presented in the Legislative Council showed. Authorities recovered only Rs 47.72 lakh, leaving over 97% unpaid, with Mysuru city recording 28,708 cases against non-police government vehicles in 2024; Home Minister Priyank Kharge said individual drivers, not the government exchequer, must pay the fines.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 4. Stop tunnel projects in Maha, Delhi, DKS dares BJP
Chief Minister D K Shivakumar on Friday defended the Bengaluru tunnel road project and challenged the BJP to stop similar tunnel road projects in Maharashtra and Delhi before criticising Karnataka's plans. Reacting to the withdrawal of the Karnataka Government Parks (Preservation) (Amendment) Bill, he denied running a U-turn government and said he is open to alternatives, noting the withdrawn amendment would have allowed alienation of up to 5% of park or garden land for infrastructure.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 5. Criminal cases to be filed against lake encroachers: Khandre
Rural Development and Panchayat Raj Minister Eshwar Khandre warned on Friday that encroachments on gram panchayat lakes will be cleared and criminal cases filed against encroachers, noting 11,498 of 32,504 lakes in gram panchayat limits have been encroached. Encroachments on 6,670 lakes covering 11,671 acres have been cleared, leaving 4,828 lakes to be recovered, as the government implements water projects for 4.45 crore people in 26,589 villages over the next 30 years.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.4_

## Top 5 — India

### 1. Mid-air scare: Probe flags system failure; AI pilot fired for drug use
An Air India Airbus A320 flying from Phuket to New Delhi with 145 people on board last month suddenly lost altitude after all three hydraulic systems collapsed within seconds before recovering, injuring two dozen fliers, the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau said in its preliminary report on Friday. The report sought action against the pilot-in-command for testing positive for drugs, calling use of a psychoactive substance by the 34-year-old pilot a serious concern, and Air India terminated his services soon after; the report did not link the pilot's drug abuse to the incident or explain why the hydraulic system failed.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.1; Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.4_

### 2. Isro's 'naughty boy' launches imaging satellite
ISRO on Friday successfully launched EOS-05, India's first imaging satellite to monitor Earth from geosynchronous orbit about 36,000 km above the planet, marking its first success nine months after the PSLV-C62 January failure in the critical third stage. GSLV-F17 lifted off at 2:55 am from Sriharikota and placed the 2,367 kg satellite into Sub-Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit 18 minutes later; Chairman Dr V Narayanan said the "eye in the sky" will provide real-time multi-band imagery for agriculture, environmental monitoring and disaster management, and noted plans to launch the Gaganyaan human spaceflight mission by year-end.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.8_

### 3. Assault on NEET protester's dad: Hindutva activist held
Hindutva activist Swatantra Bhardwaj, who claimed he cracked the skull of protester Nishu Azad's father Sanjay Kumar during the students' agitation at Jantar Mantar on June 23 and later recanted fearing legal trouble, was detained in Uttar Pradesh's Bulandshahr on Friday. The detention came hours after Delhi Police promised activists of the Cockroach Janta Party, Youth Congress and Lok Sabha MP Chandrasekhar that he would be put behind bars within 72 hours; Bhardwaj, 24, initially evaded police teams before being nabbed.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.1_

### 4. Centre to hold fresh Ladakh talks on September 9
The Central government will hold a fresh round of talks with Ladakh representatives in New Delhi on September 9, three weeks after the August 19 meeting in Leh ended without a breakthrough on the political framework sought by the Leh Apex Body and Kargil Democratic Alliance. Ladakh Chief Secretary Ashish Kundra confirmed the MHA subcommittee meeting; the groups' "non-negotiable" demands include a directly elected Legislative Assembly headed by a Chief Minister and Council of Ministers, substantial control over finances and law and order, a separate Public Service Commission, and constitutional safeguards drawing on provisions available to states such as Nagaland, Sikkim and Mizoram.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.8_

### 5. Raise judicial officers' retiring age, 7 states told
The Supreme Court directed seven consenting states—Chhattisgarh, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal—to amend service rules and raise judicial officers' retirement age from 60 to 62 years, preferably within two months, subject to high court suitability assessment at age 60. Officers who superannuated on or after March 31 this year may re-join if they have not obtained subsequent employment; the court also asked 24 other states and Union Territories to reconsider enhancing retirement age within two weeks and submit compliance reports.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.8_

## Top 5 — International

### 1. Two workers rescued from tunnel in Nepal 9 days after flash floods
Two men—Kabir Maharjan, 45, a mechanical supervisor, and Sanjay Sah, 30, a foreman—were rescued alive on Friday from the Trishuli 3A hydropower project tunnel nine days after flash floods tore through central Nepal's Trishuli River corridor. Maharjan was flown to Nepal Army Hospital in critical condition while Sah, who said he chanted the Hare Krishna mantra for nine days while trapped, is under treatment in Nuwakot; over 1,250 people have died and more than 4,700 remain missing from floods triggered by an August 26 ice-rock avalanche near the Nepal-Tibet border, with over 600 workers at 11 hydropower projects still unaccounted for.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.10; News Trail Mangaluru p.1_

### 2. Russia targets security chief's office in daylight Kyiv strike
A Russian drone struck the SBU security service headquarters on Volodymyrska Street in Kyiv on Friday, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy saying it was aimed directly at the head of the security service's personal office; acting SBU head Oleksandr Poklad was not in the vicinity. The daytime strike came two days before US envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were expected to visit Kyiv to revive peace talks, while Witkoff and Kushner are also travelling to Moscow this weekend; Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga called it a "major escalation."
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.10_

### 3. Israel emptying refugee camps crime against humanity: UN
The United Nations warned on Friday that Israel's forced displacement of more than 33,000 Palestinian refugees from Jenin, Nur Shams and Tulkarm camps during Operation "Iron Wall" launched in January 2025 in the occupied West Bank could amount to crimes against humanity of forcible transfer. The UN rights office report, slammed by Israel, described how Israeli forces killed civilians, destroyed hundreds of homes, cut water and electricity, barred humanitarian access and went door to door forcing residents to leave before consolidating control and displacing camp populations entirely.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.10_

### 4. Urine causes melting of Everest Base Camp: Study
A new study estimates about 3 million kilograms of glacial ice and snow are lost each spring at Everest Base Camp from energy used for climber comforts, with human urine alone responsible for an estimated 154 tons of melt on the Khumbu Glacier. In 2023, researchers recorded 824 cylinders of liquefied petroleum gas, 18,935 litres of kerosene and 5,130 litres of gasoline used for cooking, heating and powering about 1,600 tents, while more than 6,000 litres of urine are produced daily by climbers and guides, most of it deposited directly on the glacier rather than in pit-vault toilets.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.7_

### 5. Pig kidney keeps patient alive until human transplant
Tim Andrews, 66, received a genetically modified pig kidney at Mass General Brigham on January 25, 2025, and lived with it for nine months before becoming the first known patient to receive a human donor kidney after a pig kidney transplant. With roughly 90,000 people on the US kidney transplant waiting list and his rare blood type limiting options, Andrews' case provides early proof that pig kidneys from genetically edited animals could serve as "bridges" to human organ transplants while permanent xenotransplant solutions are developed.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.10_

## Top 5 — Sports

Smriti Mandhana smashed a 64-ball 124 with 14 fours and seven sixes against Hong Kong at Dubai to become women's international cricket's leading run-scorer with 10,880 runs, surpassing Mithali Raj's 10,868, and to register India's highest women's T20I score. The 30-year-old vice-captain now has 18 international hundreds, the most by any woman, and said she is still hungry to improve her six-hitting despite passing Mithali's tally.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.18_

### 2. Satwik-Chirag into semis; Srikanth out
Fourth-seeded Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty beat Denmark's Kim Astrup and Anders Skaarup Rasmussen 21-13, 16-21, 21-18 in 70 minutes to reach the China Masters Super 750 semifinals, while Kidambi Srikanth's men's singles campaign ended in the quarterfinals. The Indians maintained attacking rhythm after silver at the Thailand Open Super 500 and gold at the Singapore Open Super 750 in May.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.15_

### 3. India look to iron out chinks against Pakistan
Already through to the Women's Asia Cup semifinals, India face Pakistan in their final league game in Dubai on Saturday as overwhelming favourites, with Smriti Mandhana and Shafali Verma in form after Mandhana's record hundred against Hong Kong. India still seek more middle-order time from Pratika Rawal, Richa Ghosh and Harmanpreet Kaur and have added pacer Nandani Sharma, while Pakistan need batting improvement to stay in contention after beating Thailand.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.18_

### 4. Global chess stars descend in Bengaluru
The fourth Tech Mahindra Global Chess League kicks off Saturday at Hotel Ashok Lalit in Bengaluru, featuring six teams in a double round-robin Rapid format with Magnus Carlsen, Viswanathan Anand and Alexandra Kosteniuk among the stars. Carlsen rejoins defending champions Alpine APL Pipers; Anand is icon for PBG Alaskan Knights with 15-year-old Yagiz Kaan Erdogmus and Arjun Erigaisi; wins with Black earn four points and wins with White three.

### 5. India to play Japan in historic T20I contest
India's men's team will play Japan in their first-ever T20 International on September 22 at Sano International Cricket Ground in Tochigi Prefecture, launching celebrations for the 75th anniversary of India-Japan diplomatic relations and offering game time before the Asian Games in Aichi-Nagoya from September 19 to October 4. The sides will compete for the Suzuki Cup, the BCCI and Japan Cricket Association announced.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.18_
