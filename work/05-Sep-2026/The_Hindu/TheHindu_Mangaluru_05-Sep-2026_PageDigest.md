### The people who teach us to become (Opinion)

India marks Teachers' Day on September 5, the birth anniversary of philosopher and former President Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, who is said to have asked students to celebrate all teachers instead of him alone. The column argues that classrooms are a child's first real society outside home, that Radhakrishnan saw education as building reason and democratic character rather than rote memorisation, and that teaching today is a complex profession—UNESCO's recent AI campaign stresses technology cannot replace a human paying attention. It contends teachers need more than annual cards and speeches: time, training, resources, autonomy and respect, as their influence persists in how people question, choose and contribute long after school ends.

### Warning on warming (Opinion)

The United Nations Environment Programme's Limiting Overshoot report warns that breaching the 1.5°C global warming limit would bring irreversible losses that adaptation cannot undo; even if all countries deliver on climate pledges today, the globe is still poised to heat 1.8°C, and on current policies 2.6°C. The report describes an "overshoot, peak and decline" pathway and emphasises action on methane — responsible for about 0.5°C of current warming — as the most effective near-term lever; over 155 countries have joined the Global Methane Pledge to cut anthropogenic methane at least 30% below 2020 levels by 2030, but India is not among them. After the Bhotekoshi disaster in Nepal, Foreign Affairs Minister Shisir Khanal said major industrial emitters such as China, the United States and India must consider the impact of rising temperatures on small countries; the editorial argues India must take such perceptions seriously and calibrate its messaging to the reality of a 1.5°C world.

### The Gulf is calling and New Delhi must listen (Opinion)
