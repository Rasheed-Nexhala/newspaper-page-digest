### Bar Council has no power to punish law students: SC

The Supreme Court on Thursday declared that the Bar Council of India and its Chairperson Manan Kumar Mishra lack jurisdiction to discipline law students, rebuking attempts to intimidate NALSAR students who protested CJI Kant as convocation chief guest after his "cockroaches" comments. A three-judge Bench headed by CJI Surya Kant held that the Advocates Act, 1961, does not confer power on the BCI or State Bar Councils to take disciplinary action against law students — that authority rests with universities, and the BCI can step in only after enrolment. The BCI had revoked its August 13 letters to NALSAR and State Bar Councils.

### Youth power, an electoral force still in the making (Opinion)

Akshay Rout writes that while Gen Z street protests have forced parties to engage with youth issues, India’s largest youth cohort has not yet consolidated into a predictable electoral bloc, with no discernible youth voting trend in recent Assembly polls in Tamil Nadu, Assam, West Bengal, Keralam and Puducherry. About 65% of India’s population is below 35 and roughly 23% of the electorate is aged 18–29, but caste, community and gender identities have largely subsumed youth identity, and upcoming polls in Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Manipur and Goa will test whether street noise translates into organised political preference.

### For newborns, the answer is hospital plus home (Opinion)

Abhay Bang argues that rising institutional deliveries — from 39% in 2005–06 to 90% in 2023–24 — have overcrowded Special Newborn Care Units, contributing to clustered newborn deaths including three killed in an August 24, 2026 fire at Amravati’s government women’s hospital. Citing the Gadchiroli trial’s 62.2% neonatal mortality reduction through home-based neonatal care by trained ASHAs, he advocates a three-part strategy of decongesting units via strengthened home care, improving facility staffing and safety, and ensuring not every newborn who needs care requires a hospital bed.
