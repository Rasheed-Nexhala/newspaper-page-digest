### NUMBER 1 — Proposal for reconstruction of Karambar bridge

Number one. The PWD sent a Rs 2 crore reconstruction proposal in March 2025 after a portion of the 1963 arch bridge sank on Wednesday on the MIA–Bajpe SH-67.

A temporary earthen culvert with concrete pipes allows light vehicles while heavy vehicles use alternate roads. City–airport traffic is unaffected as the bridge lies beyond MIA.

**Host take**

If you use the Bajpe–airport corridor, this bridge sits on that route — and officials are now looking at a full rebuild after part of the old structure gave way.

---

### NUMBER 2 — Railways to install double boom barrier at Pandeshwar Level Crossing

Number two. Palakkad Division, pending a road-over-bridge, will install a double boom-barrier at Pandeshwar Level Crossing at about Rs 2 crore.

Tenders are open and work is expected to be finalised within a fortnight and completed in six months. MCC and the DC offered to widen a 200 metre road stretch to four lanes when Railways widens the crossing; MLA Vedavyasa Kamath and MP Brijesh Chowta pursued the issue.

**Host take**

This is a level crossing many Mangaluru commuters know — and Railways is putting roughly Rs 2 crore into a double boom-barrier while the road-over-bridge plan is still pending.

---

### NUMBER 3 — Street vendors evicted in Hampankatta

Number three. Mangaluru City Corporation officials launched an eviction drive on Thursday evening against street vendors who had illegally encroached footpaths in Hampankatta and surrounding areas, removing flowers and temporary structures and warning of stringent legal action against repeat encroachment.

Dakshina Kannada Zilla Beedhibadhi Vyaparasthara Sangha Honorary President B K Imthiyaz condemned the drive, saying flower vendors from Hassan, Mandya and other districts had come for Krishnashtami and that the Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014 permits vending during festivals.

**Host take**

This is a straight clash between the corporation's footpath-clearance drive and traders who say festival-season vending is protected under the 2014 street vendors law.

---

### NUMBER 4 — Forest staff seek divine intervention to tackle elephant menace

Number four. Belthangady forest department staff offered Rangapooja at the Sowthadka Shree Mahaganapathi kshetra in Kokkada, praying that wild elephants would not cause loss of human life or damage to agriculture.

During 2026-27, over 80 wild elephants destroyed crops and areca plantations worth over Rs 77 lakh in Belthangady, Sullia and Kadaba taluks. Rs 3.30 lakh compensation was released to 19 people while Rs 73 lakh remained pending for 381, and forest officials have submitted a proposal to establish an elephant camp at Gundya.

**Host take**

Rs 77 lakh in crop damage and Rs 73 lakh in compensation still pending for hundreds of farmers — that's the scale of the problem behind the prayers and the Gundya camp proposal.

---

### NUMBER 5 — Case filed against associate NCC officers of NITK over financial irregularities

Number five. Surathkal Police registered a case against NITK Associate NCC Officers Prof P Johnson and Prof H Shivananda after an internal Comptroller and Auditor General audit found TA/DA claim bills were based on false documents. The duo were detained and an internal investigation is underway.

NITK Director B Ravi said the NCC unit must uphold morality and integrity, and appointed Dr Mohan Lal Meena, Assistant Professor in Chemical Engineering, as NCC caretaker officer in place of the ANOs.

**Host take**

This is about travel-and-dearness claims at NITK's NCC unit — flagged by a CAG audit, now a police case and a leadership change at Surathkal.
