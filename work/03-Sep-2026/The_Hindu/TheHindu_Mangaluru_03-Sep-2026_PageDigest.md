### UN charts course to manage global warming-limit breach

Warning that a breach of the 1.5 degrees Celsius global warming limit will bring irreversible ecological losses, the United Nations Environment Programme on Wednesday set out a detailed "overshoot, peak, and decline" pathway for the first time. The Limiting Overshoot report said global warming would cross 1.5 degrees Celsius in the next few years; even if every country delivers on its national climate plan and net-zero target, peak warming would reach 1.8 degrees Celsius, while current policies point to a rise of about 2.6 degrees Celsius by 2100.

### Traffic between Mangaluru and Bajpe affected as minor bridge becomes unstable

Vehicular movement between Mangaluru and Bajpe on Mangaluru-Athradi State Highway 67 was affected after an old minor bridge built in 1963 at Karambar, between MIA and Bajpe, became unstable following the collapse of a portion of its arch on Wednesday. Traffic between the city and the airport was not affected as the damaged bridge lies beyond MIA; those travelling from Bajpe to the airport and on the city–Kateel pilgrim route were disturbed, and the district administration advised road users to use Porkodi–Jokatte to reach the city and Jarinagara to reach the airport. PWD engineers had sent reconstruction proposals years ago without government approval and will now send an emergency proposal; after temporary restoration, vehicles other than very heavy ones could ply until a new bridge is constructed.

### Formulate rules for fishing vessel transit to EEZ, SC directs T.N.

The Supreme Court on Wednesday directed Tamil Nadu to frame rules creating a "specified channel" or official navigation corridor under the Marine Fishing Regulation Rules 2020 for transit of vessels from territorial waters to the EEZ for purse seine fishing. A Division Bench of Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe upheld both the state's Marine Fishing Regulation Rules 2020 and the Centre's Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the EEZ Rules 2025, noting the EEZ Rules contemplate an "access pass," after the state raised concern that fishers could employ purse seine nets within territorial waters under the guise of EEZ fishing. The court held that rights and liabilities are governed by the relevant laws and said there is no conflict between the Union's EEZ Rules and Tamil Nadu's marine fishery regulation rules.

### Many layers (Opinion)

Since the 1960s India has balanced affordable food prices with remunerative farmer prices. The government banned onion exports from December 2023 to May 2024, imposed a $550/tonne minimum export price and a 40% export duty—reduced to 20% in September 2024 and abolished in April 2025. During a bounteous rabi harvest, onion farmers in Maharashtra argued the Centre's procurement price of ₹12.35/kg would not cover cultivation costs; it was raised to up to ₹26.45/kg, but many farmers could not capture that value, including some who sold at ₹1/kg. Abnormal rainfall at harvest and a 5%–7% drop in the kharif crop in Maharashtra followed; Tamil Nadu targeted a subsidy to buy 1,000 tonnes of onions and distribute 1 kg per ration card at ₹35.

### 'Early Harvest' — larger but not necessarily safer (Opinion)

At August 25, 2026 special representative talks in Beijing, an August 26 "Eight Points" document restored "Early and Substantial Harvest" for boundary delimitation and border management—India's coinage from around 2019 as a counter to China's Sikkim-only proposal. A genuine Early Harvest requires highest-watershed delineation, trijunction points excluded per the 2012 Common Understanding, and terms of reference extending India's 2019 proposal. The Eight Points are silent on the Medog County project on the Yarlung Tsangpo; a September meeting should convey India's concerns about the world's largest hydropower plant near the border in an earthquake-prone zone.

### Reducing India's exposure to U.S. tariff risks (Opinion)

The U.S. Senate passed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act 2026, with tariffs up to 100% on countries among the five largest importers of Russian crude if they make new purchases after enactment. Russian crude was roughly 2% of India's imports before the Russia-Ukraine conflict and is now about half; 2026 imports nearly doubled from 4.54 MMT in January to 8.96 MMT in May. On July 24 the U.S. imposed forced-labour tariffs of 10% on 60 countries including India. ### Can jurists be appointed as Supreme Court judges?

Supreme Court judge Justice Ujjal Bhuyan questioned at NLU Delhi's 13th LL.M. convocation why Article 124(3)'s route for appointing a 'distinguished jurist' as a Supreme Court judge has remained unused for more than 76 years, noting no legal academic has been elevated under the provision while judges have overwhelmingly come from High Court benches and only 11 advocates were directly elevated from the Bar. The explainer traces Constituent Assembly intent to diversify the Bench, the rejected and later omitted High Court jurist provision, and barriers including Collegium recommendation and restrictions on full-time law teachers practising as advocates.

### A colonial gaze: how the British imagined Bastar and its people

An excerpt from Shantanu Nandan Sharma's Rebel Bastar describes how colonial accounts portrayed Bastar as a primitive woodland of cannibals — including Henry Harpur Spry's claim that its people 'sacrifice and eat their fellow creatures' without ever visiting — while flawed censuses produced wildly unreliable figures: the 1866 headcount recorded 2,69,684 people but the 1872 census showed an inexplicable 70% drop to 78,856, with J.W. Neill noting children were not enumerated in many villages and enumerators recorded colourful but inaccurate professions such as 'con man', 'pimp', and 'idiot'.
