### IMD vs KSNDMC: Why Karnataka has two rainfall deficit figures

Karnataka's rainfall deficit figures differ between IMD, which reported a 26% shortfall, and KSNDMC, which puts it at 32%, because the agencies use different rain-gauge networks and baseline periods. IMD relies on 419 stations while KSNDMC operates over 5,000 active gauges across hobli and gram panchayat levels. IMD Bengaluru head N Puviarasan said differences in normals may converge over time; KSNDMC Director M S Divakara said Karnataka's 25–30 years of data led the Union government to declare the state drought-affected in 2023.

### Is India overpaying for its semiconductor dream?

India's Semicon 2.0 adds Rs 1,27,500 crore ($13.3 billion) to an initial Rs 76,000 crore mission, offering up to 50% capital subsidies for fabs as New Delhi tries to catch up in the global chip race against the US CHIPS Act's $52.7 billion and Europe's €43 billion Chips Act. Industry assessments note imports account for about 85% of initial fab setup costs, talent gaps could leave subsidised plants underutilised, and former RBI Governor Raghuram Rajan has questioned whether subsidising multiple generations of chip factories is sustainable if market cycles turn before facilities reach capacity.

### Strong law, weak delivery

Despite RERA passing orders in favour of aggrieved homebuyers, advocates and industry experts say weak enforcement often leaves buyers in long legal battles through appellate tribunals and courts, while developers may use insolvency proceedings under the IBC to stall execution. ### The semiconductor race

India's 1976 Semiconductor Complex Limited in Mohali was manufacturing 5-nanometer chips by 1984 before a 1989 fire and bureaucratic delays stalled momentum, making today's multi-billion-dollar subsidies the price of lost silicon sovereignty. Imports account for about 85% of initial fab setup costs, Micron's $2.75 billion Sanand ATMP recently shifted its engineering contract from Tata Projects to L&T, and Deloitte estimates India needs four to five lakh specialised semiconductor professionals annually. Former RBI Governor Raghuram Rajan warned subsidy sizes will keep rising for successive chip generations, while India brings Micron and Kaynes Semicon facilities into production and expands Semicon 2.0 support for specialty gases, chemicals and chip design.
