### Glacial collapse unlikely to halt China's plan to build mega dam

Fresh concerns triggered by the devastating glacial collapse along the Nepal-China border are unlikely to sway Beijing's plans to build a mega dam on the lower reaches of the Yarlung Zangbo, or Brahmaputra river, in a seismically active region in Tibet near the border with India. China's 15th Five-Year Plan for renewable energy, released on July 23, calls for advancing the Yarlung Zangbo Lower Reaches Hydropower Project; Vice Premier Zhang Guoqing visited Nyingchi in April and Premier Li Qiang announced construction start in July 2025, calling it "the project of the century" with total investment of 1.2 trillion Yuan (around ₹14 lakh crore) for five power stations. Of concern to India is how much water will be stored in the reservoir and how the tunnel will affect downstream flows, as well as possible broader ecological impacts; the lack of information has posed an obstacle in assessing the possible impact.

### Voter lists shrink over 15% since 2024 elections in States, U.T.s where SIR third-phase draft lists are out

Since the 2024 Lok Sabha elections, voter lists of 17 States and Union Territories where Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is under way in its third phase have shrunk by 15.7% as per draft rolls published by the Election Commission, from 35,50,50,008 to 29,93,00,115. Maharashtra recorded the largest drop in absolute numbers, losing 15,896,198 electors or 17.08% in the draft phase, while Delhi and Chandigarh registered the sharpest dips in percentage at 35.89% and 31.84% respectively. These States and U.T.s had added around 57.75 lakh voters after the polls, taking the cumulative total to 36,08,07,394, but 6.15 crore voters have been deleted as per the draft rolls.

### Glacial collapse unlikely to halt China's dam plan

China has already opened the Zangmu dam on the middle reaches of the Yarlung Zangbo, which a state media report said had provided a solution to nearly 26% of electricity demand in Southwest China's Xizang (Tibet) Autonomous Region and included an elaborate fishway for future projects. In June, a final acceptance meeting declared the dam, which started construction in 2010, had generated 22.2 billion kilowatt-hours of energy. The new project near the India border at the "great bend" is expected to dwarf Zangmu, with Chinese analysts projecting 60 GW installed capacity and 300 billion kilowatt-hours of power a year; though described as run-of-the-river, it will involve a reservoir likely at Mainling, with water diverted through an approximately 40-km tunnel to five 12-GW powerhouses. India and China will hold an Expert-Level Mechanism on Transborder Rivers meeting later this month, though hydrological data sharing has not been regular; China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said Beijing maintains a highly responsible attitude and that the project is beneficial for disaster prevention without adverse downstream effects.
