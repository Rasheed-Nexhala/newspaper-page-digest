Headline: India, Uzbekistan upgrade ties, agree on uranium deal

Section: TASHKENT | Press Trust of India

Body: India and Uzbekistan on Sunday decided to elevate their ties to a comprehensive strategic partnership and set a target of $5 billion in annual trade by 2030 following talks between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev.

The two sides inked 11 agreements for boosting engagement in several key sectors, including mining and tourism, and agreed to establish a framework for long-term supply of uranium from Uzbekistan to India in line with cooperation in the civil nuclear energy sector.

The talks focused on expanding cooperation in areas of trade, investment, infrastructure, critical minerals, mining, defence and security, agriculture, pharmaceuticals, health, information technology, ayurveda, digital public infrastructure, including UPI, and education.

"We are celebrating the 15th anniversary of our strategic partnership. I am delighted that, on this special occasion, we are elevating our ties to the level of a comprehensive strategic partnership," Mr. Modi said.

"We will move forward by setting concrete goals within this framework," he said. Mr. Modi said both sides would also elevate their existing joint commission from the level of secretaries to that of ministerial level.

Mr. Mirziyoyev said Uzbekistan was determined to boost ties with India in diverse sectors. "We are aiming to advance cooperation in sectors such as energy, culture, critical minerals, pharmaceuticals, jewellery, and agriculture," the Uzbek President said. The current volume of bilateral trade between the two nations is over $1 billion.

"Uzbekistan is at the heart of Central Asia and is at the heart of India's engagement in the region," Mr. Modi said.

Headline: 28 from Karnataka stranded or missing after Nepal floods

Section: BENGALURU | The Hindu Bureau

Body: At least 28 people from Karnataka have been reported stranded or missing in Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR) following flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border and adjoining areas, according to government sources.

23 from Bengaluru

Of the 28 persons, 23 are from Bengaluru, two from Mysuru, and one each from Dharwad, Udupi, and Vijayanagara districts.

Most of those stranded or missing are believed to be in Timure in Nepal's Rasuwa district, while a few are in Darchen in the Tibet Autonomous Region.

The State Emergency Operations Centre (SEOC) said four of the 28 people have been confirmed safe and are currently transiting to Nepal. Search and tracing operations are under way to locate the remaining 24 persons. Several people landed at Kempegowda International Airport on Saturday.

The Karnataka government said Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar and Deputy Chief Minister and Revenue Minister G. Parameshwara are in constant touch with the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) and the Resident Commissioner at Karnataka Bhavan in New Delhi to facilitate the rescue and safe return of those from the State.

The Resident Commissioner, Karnataka Bhavan, has also constituted dedicated teams to track the situation and coordinate with authorities concerned.

The Indian Embassy in Beijing has also opened two 24-hour helplines in the Tibet Autonomous Region to assist families seeking information about missing persons.

Headline: T.N. moves SC, seeks release of Cauvery water backlog

Section: NEW DELHI | Krishnadas Rajagopal

Body: The Tamil Nadu government approached the Supreme Court on Sunday, seeking a direction to Karnataka to release 17.604 tmcft of water from the Cauvery, the balance quantity as on August 27, based on the pro-rata formula.

The State, in a supplementary affidavit settled by senior advocate G. Umamathy and re-settled by C.S. Vaidyanathan, also urged the court to order Karnataka to release 9,000 cusecs of water between August 26 and September 8, as directed by the Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA).

It further sought the top court's intervention to direct Karnataka to make good the shortfall between the flows ensured at Billigundulu, a riverside village on the border between the two States, and the flows that the CWMA had directed to take place between August 27 and August 30.

"The farmers of Cauvery Delta are seriously prejudiced as Kuruvai season is almost over and Mettur (dam) could not be open for irrigation," Tamil Nadu submitted in the affidavit.

A Bench is scheduled to hear the Cauvery case on August 31.

Tamil Nadu said that CWRC had fixed 9,000 cusecs for the next 15 days with effect from August 25, without considering the accumulated backlog of 17.415 tmc as on August 23.

Tamil Nadu said it had raised its grievance before the CWMA, at its meeting the very next day on August 25, about the "gross inadequacy" of the CWRC order for release of water.

The examination was held on January 8, 2026, and the final selection list was released on July 17. Following allegations of malpractice, the CID sent 329 OMR sheets of candidates who appeared for the examination to the State Forensic Science Laboratory for examination.

Mr. Gangwar's arrest follows days of scrutiny after the Directorate of Enforcement (ED) conducted searches. He had subsequently appeared before the CID and was questioned extensively regarding his tenure as Controller of Examinations.

The CID has also questioned suspended KPSC Chairman Shivashankarappa S. Sahukar and several other officials.

Headline: Trails of Kudremukh Hills are alive with Neelakurinji-like bloom

Section: BENGALURU | The Hindu Bureau

Body: Adding a distinctive charm to the beauty of the Western Ghats, a rare blue-violet flower resembling Neelakurinji has bloomed big along some trekking trails in the Kudremukh region of Chikkamagaluru, attracting more trekkers and nature enthusiasts.

According to the Forest and Tourism Department officials, these flowers were seen in greater abundance in some of the hill regions of Kudremukh during 2022–23, earlier. They have appeared in limited numbers again this time.

The blue-violet blooms scattered across the green hills are adding a unique attraction to the natural landscape of Kudremukh.

Sampath, owner of Namma Mane homestay near Kalasa in Chikkamagaluru, said that the bloom in 2022-23 had attracted big crowds to the region and they were expecting a similar jump in tourist traffic this season too.

Ram Prasath Manohar, Secretary, Tourism Department, said that the limited blooming of these blue-violet flowers in Kudremukh this year has generated special interest among trekking enthusiasts and nature tourists. "There is a need to present such natural phenomena through environmentally responsible tourism. Instructions have been given to the district Tourism Department officials to provide visitors with the necessary information, guidance and facilities without causing any harm to the flowers or their habitat," he said.

He urged the visitors not to pluck the flowers and only watch them. "If they are plucked, then it disturbs the blooming cycle of these flowers. We have requested visitors not to damage the plants or litter the surroundings and help us preserve the natural environment," Mr. Manohar said.

Tourism Minister K.J. George said blue-violet flowers like neelakurinji showcase the exceptional beauty of Karnataka's natural wealth. "While visiting such attractions, tourists must adopt eco-friendly travel practices," he said.
