### India, Uzbekistan upgrade ties, agree on uranium deal

India and Uzbekistan on Sunday decided to elevate their ties to a comprehensive strategic partnership and set a target of $5 billion in annual trade by 2030 following talks between Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev. ### 28 from Karnataka stranded or missing after Nepal floods

At least 28 people from Karnataka have been reported stranded or missing in Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR) following flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border and adjoining areas, according to government sources; of them, 23 are from Bengaluru, two from Mysuru, and one each from Dharwad, Udupi, and Vijayanagara districts. Most are believed to be in Timure in Nepal's Rasuwa district or in Darchen in TAR; the State Emergency Operations Centre said four of the 28 have been confirmed safe and are transiting to Nepal, while search and tracing operations continue for the remaining 24. Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar and Deputy Chief Minister and Revenue Minister G. Parameshwara are in constant touch with the Ministry of External Affairs and Karnataka Bhavan in New Delhi, and the Indian Embassy in Beijing has opened two 24-hour helplines in TAR.

### India must not normalise a culture of discrimination (Opinion)

In its first review by the U.N. Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination (CERD) since 2007, India has elicited an expression of "grave concern" about reports of law enforcement resorting to violence against minority ethnic and ethno-religious groups, Dalits, and non-citizens, and about the lack of detailed updated information on inquiries and sanctions arising from such allegations. CERD's concerns span manual sewer cleaning despite a ban, hate speech and poor conditions for Rohingya Muslims, mass refoulement, citizenship deprivation via the NCR, electoral roll deletions, use of FCRA, UAPA, AFSPA and PMLA against civil society, the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita not expressly criminalising racist hate speech, and Forest (Conservation) Amendment Act exemptions; delayed Census and NCRB data are said to impede independent checks on discrimination claims, while the Global Alliance of National Human Rights Institutions recommended downgrading the NHRC last year. The editorial argues India must restore NHRC independence, criminalise hate speech, publish disaggregated data, improve electoral roll transparency, and strengthen enforcement, rather than dismiss CERD's concerns as politically motivated and treat Dalit issues as a domestic matter beyond international accountability.
