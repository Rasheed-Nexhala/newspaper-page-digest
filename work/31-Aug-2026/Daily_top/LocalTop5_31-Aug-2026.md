## Top 5 — Mangaluru

### 1. Only 97 accident victims obtain benefits under PM-RAHAT scheme in DK district

Only 97 road accident victims in Dakshina Kannada have availed treatment under the Centre's PM-RAHAT scheme since its launch in February this year, with officials attributing the low uptake to lack of public awareness and reluctance among some hospitals. The scheme provides cashless treatment of up to Rs 1.5 lakh for up to seven days from the date of a road accident for all victims injured in motor vehicle accidents, irrespective of APL or BPL status; District Health Officer Dr HR Thimmaiah said notices would be issued to reluctant hospitals, and Dakshina Kannada MP Capt Brijesh Chowta directed effective implementation at a DISHA meeting.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.1_

### 2. Vande Bharat service set for introduction after ghat trial

The Research, Design and Standards Organisation (RDSO) successfully conducted trial runs for a Vande Bharat Express between Bengaluru and Mangaluru on the Sakleshpur-Subrahmanya Road ghat section of the Hassan-Mangaluru line from August 12 to 19, using a 20-car rake fitted with an Automatic Emergency Brake (AEB) system to prevent speeds above 30 kmph on the 1-in-50 gradient stretch. South Western Railway's Mysuru division is awaiting a 16-car AEB-equipped rake from Integral Coach Factory, Chennai, while the Ministry of Railways has yet to declare the final route, timings, and halts; the service is expected to take at least eight hours end to end compared with nine-and-a-half hours for conventional trains.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6_

### 3. Special SIR campaign in DK on Sept 19, 20

As part of the ongoing Special Intensive Revision (SIR) in Dakshina Kannada district, notices seeking voter verification have begun to be generated, with a total of 1,50,144 notices planned across the district; 25,389 have been generated so far, 1,24,532 are pending, and 16 have been delivered. A total of 154 Additional Assistant Electoral Registration Officers have been appointed, and a special campaign for voter registration will be conducted on September 19 and 20.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.1_

### 4. Mescom recruits 395 powermen to ease shortage of field staff

Mangalore Electricity Supply Company (MESCOM) has recruited 395 powermen, with 165 more to be recruited through KPTCL shortly, said Mescom Chairman K Harish Kumar; of the 395 recruited recently, only five are from Dakshina Kannada district and fewer than 50 are from areas under Mescom jurisdiction. Mescom will convert power lines in elephant corridors and forest areas into covered conductor systems in Puttur, Shivamogga, Sagar, Chikkamagaluru-1 and Chikkamagaluru-2, Koppa and Kundapura divisions, procure 705 non-contact voltage detectors, lay underground cables in Ullal, Mannagudda and Kavoor sub-divisions, and reported a Rs 25.77 crore loss from April 1 to August 26 after 17,770 electricity poles and 496.37 km of power lines were damaged.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.1_

### 5. Santhosh Kumar Shetty appointed MUDA chairman

Santhosh Kumar Shetty has been appointed MUDA chairman by the government; the tenure of previous chairman Sadashiva Ullal had ended in February and was later extended by six months.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.1_

## Top 5 — Coastal Karnataka

### 1. Hindu groups to hold 'Puttur Chalo' rally today over police handling of clash

Several Hindu organisations plan a "Puttur Chalo" rally on Monday from Darbe to the Venkataramana Temple in Puttur over alleged poor police handling of a recent clash outside a photocopying centre, with conditional police approval and heavy deployment including seven District Armed Reserve units, five Karnataka State Reserve Police units, and two Special Action Force units. CPI(M) district secretary Muneer Katipalla urged people not to participate, alleging the BJP and Sangh Parivar were giving communal colour to a simple customer-shopkeeper clash to disturb peace and target Superintendent of Police K. Arun.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.1_

### 2. 24 fishermen rescued from sinking boat in Karwar

All 24 fishermen aboard the 'Shree Mata' vessel were rescued on Sunday after the boat overturned a few nautical miles off Anjadeev Island near Karwar; a hole at the bottom may have caused flooding. The boat belonging to Neelakanta Karvy of Ankola sank despite rescue efforts by nearby fishing crews, fisheries officials said.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.3_

### 3. Four get jail terms for defaming SC couple

A court in Udupi sentenced Shobha and her daughter Chaitra of Alevoor village and their friends Bhaskar Shetty and Bharath Ganiga to six months' simple imprisonment each under Section 504 of the IPC and under Sections 3(1)(b), 3(1)(s), and 3(1)(z) of the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, with Bhaskar and Bharath additionally sentenced to nine months under Section 506 IPC read with Section 3(1)(r) of the SC/ST Act, all sentences to run concurrently. Complainant Panjima alleged the four defamed her and her husband, abused them over their caste, and threw waste and liquor bottles in front of their house, continuing casteist abuse when questioned in December 2019.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6_

### 4. Sullia MLA urges govt. to curb elephant menace

Sullia MLA Bhagirathi Murulya told a District Development Co-ordination and Monitoring Committee meeting on Saturday that elephants in and around Sullia have become a major problem for farmers and urged the government to protect farms, calling for the State to bear the full cost of solar fencing instead of the current 50% subsidy. An official said around 80 elephants are currently roaming the Sullia and Puttur areas.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6_

### 5. Byndoor railway station to be redeveloped at ₹100 crore: MP

Shivamogga MP B.Y. Raghavendra said Mookambika Road Byndoor Railway Station in Udupi district will be developed as a full-fledged station at around ₹100 crore under the Mookambika Corridor concept, with the Railway Board recently approving the plan as part of Konkan Railway Corporation Ltd's third financial restructuring. The upgrade includes a modern station building, conversion to a block station with three loop lines and a separate goods shed line, full-length platform shelters, refurbished flooring, and a coach watering facility; Raghavendra said he has asked Minister of State for Railways V. Somanna to declare Byndoor the official station for Kollur.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.6_

## Top 5 — Karnataka

### 1. 'Save Lalbagh' cry grows louder with massive rally

Thousands gathered at Lalbagh Botanical Garden on Sunday demanding withdrawal of the Karnataka Government Parks (Preservation) (Amendment) Bill, 2026, which allows up to 5% of park land statewide for public infrastructure. Protesters at the 'Walk for Lalbagh' campaign raised 'Save Lalbagh' slogans and warned that a planned tunnel road passing under Lalbagh threatens the park's geology and ecosystem. Environmentalist A N Yellappa Reddy launched a signature campaign, comparing park destruction to harming Bengaluru's vital organs.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.14; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.2; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.2; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.2_

### 2. KPSC 'scam': Gyanendra Kumar Gangwar arrested by CID

The Criminal Investigation Department (CID) of the Karnataka Police on Saturday arrested IAS officer Gyanendra Kumar Gangwar in connection with alleged irregularities in the recruitment of 400 veterinary officers, making him the first accused to be arrested; he served as Controller of Examinations at the Karnataka Public Service Commission (KPSC) from February 2024 to March 2026. Investigators have allegedly found prima facie evidence including suspected manipulation of OMR sheets; the examination was held on January 8, 2026, the final selection list was released on July 17, and 329 OMR sheets were sent to the State Forensic Science Laboratory; he was remanded in judicial custody on Sunday evening and the CID will likely seek his custody on Monday.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.4; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.14_

### 3. Govt. panel to review withdrawal of criminal cases

The State government has constituted a six-member Cabinet sub-committee, headed by Home Minister Priyank Kharge, to examine criminal cases pending before various courts in the State and recommend cases that may be withdrawn. The committee comprises Social Welfare Minister K. H. Muniyappa, Health and Family Welfare and Minority Welfare, Haj and Wakf Minister U.T. Khader, Greater Bengaluru Development Minister Krishna Byre Gowda, Municipal Administration Minister H. C. Balakrishna, and Urban Development Minister Yathindra Siddaramaiah, and while considering each case will examine its nature, background and other relevant factors.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.14_

### 4. 28 from Karnataka stranded or missing after Nepal floods

At least 28 people from Karnataka have been reported stranded or missing in Nepal and the Tibet Autonomous Region (TAR) following flash floods along the Nepal-Tibet border and adjoining areas, according to government sources; of them, 23 are from Bengaluru, two from Mysuru, and one each from Dharwad, Udupi, and Vijayanagara districts. Most are believed to be in Timure in Nepal's Rasuwa district or in Darchen in TAR; the State Emergency Operations Centre said four of the 28 have been confirmed safe and are transiting to Nepal, while search and tracing operations continue for the remaining 24. Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar and Deputy Chief Minister and Revenue Minister G. Parameshwara are in constant touch with the Ministry of External Affairs and Karnataka Bhavan in New Delhi, and the Indian Embassy in Beijing has opened two 24-hour helplines in TAR.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5_

### 5. Trekking: Forest dept rolls out stricter SOP

The Forest Department introduced a comprehensive SOP to regulate trekking across Karnataka's nearly 39 designated routes, mandating route-wise safety assessments, carrying capacity limits, trained local guides and GPS tracking in areas with poor connectivity. Trekking may be suspended where tigers, leopards, elephants or bears are active, following 2026 incidents including a fatal leopard attack at MM Hills and a missing trekker search at Tadiandamol in Kodagu.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.2_

## Top 5 — India

### 1. India provides 68.5 tonnes of relief, tunnel expertise

India has provided 68.5 tonnes of relief materials to flood-hit Nepal and sent technical teams, forensic experts and equipment for search and rescue, the Indian embassy said. A specialised contingent including tunnel experts was deployed to hydropower tunnels where workers are believed trapped after Wednesday's flash floods, and a fourth flight carrying 11 tonnes of relief landed on Sunday, the Ministry of External Affairs said. India has also sent a forensic team to work with Nepali counterparts on DNA sample analysis; Nepal is also liaising with a team from China.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.11_

### 2. India close to long-term uranium import deal with Uzbekistan

Modi and Uzbek President Mirziyoyev agreed to explore uranium and critical-minerals cooperation, potentially beyond buyer-seller ties as India seeks overseas fuel for nuclear expansion. Cameco will supply nearly 22 million pounds of uranium concentrate between 2027 and 2035, Australia arrangements were finalised in July, and bilateral trade targets were set at $5 billion by 2030 from over $1 billion now.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.4; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.14; The Hindu Mangaluru p.12_

### 3. TN files fresh plea in SC, says K'taka not following CWMA orders

Tamil Nadu filed a fresh Supreme Court plea seeking directions for Karnataka to release the balance 17.604 tmcft of Cauvery water on a pro-rata basis as on August 27, 2026, and 9,000 cusec as directed by CWMA from August 26 to September 8. Tamil Nadu contended Karnataka reduced releases after the August 25 CWMA order, severely affecting Cauvery Delta farmers; the court had fixed the matter for Monday, August 31, 2026.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.3; The Hindu Mangaluru p.5_

### 4. No trace of 61 pilgrims from Maharashtra

There was no news till Sunday of 61 Mumbai pilgrims, including Dr Jay Kotecha, missing since August 26 Nepal flash floods during a Hills and Trials/Richa Tours Kailash Mansarovar trip that left Mumbai on August 23 and was due back September 6. Families contacted the Centre and Maharashtra government as relatives sought army-led evacuation after pilgrims were last reachable near the Gyirong border immigration complex.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.4_

### 5. Alarmed by Nepal disaster, experts plan Arunachal glacier survey to find ways to reduce GLOF impact

After Nepal's glacier-linked devastation, experts from IIT-Roorkee, IIT-Mandi, NCPOR and CESHS Arunachal Pradesh plan a September bathymetric survey of the Khangri Glacier in Tawang to assess glacial lakes and reduce GLOF/LLOF risk. CESHS director Tana Tage said the Eastern Himalayas' retreating glaciers and expanding lakes pose major downstream water-security and disaster risks for the Brahmaputra basin.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.4_

## Top 5 — International

### 1. 788 dead, over 2,500 still missing in Nepal

Four days after the flood in Nepal, the death toll in the disaster touched 788, and 2,502 people remained missing even as authorities continued rescue efforts and faced challenges preserving recovered bodies. Foreign Secretary Amrit Bahadur Rai said Nepal urgently required freezer units and DNA-testing kits, warning that bodies must be preserved and identified quickly to prevent decomposition and contain public-health risks, and that the government had requested assistance from several countries.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.5; The Hindu Mangaluru p.12_

### 2. Authorities step up efforts to rescue hundreds trapped in tunnels

Nepal's military airlifted heavy machinery by helicopter on Sunday to an under-construction dam as rescuers battled rising waters to reach workers trapped in a tunnel four days after floods. The China-Nepal border disaster killed more than 780 people, left more than 3,000 missing, knocked out about 10% of Nepal's power generation and inundated several hydropower projects. Rescue operations are underway at five hydroelectric projects including the Upper Trishuli complex, where authorities said around 350 people could be trapped; about 10,000 Nepali troops are deployed alongside specialists from China and India. Dharam Raj Uprety, chief of Nepal's national disaster management authority, said debris makes it very tough to blast a way through.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.11_

### 3. Tensions spike between Türkiye and Israel

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan has called Israel a "terror state," compared Benjamin Netanyahu to Hitler and floated Turkish military intervention, while Netanyahu has branded Erdogan an "antisemitic dictator" and accused Türkiye of acting like an enemy state, fuelling a rivalry analysts say could destabilise West Asia. On August 18 Israel bombed a defunct Syrian air base at Abu al-Duhur to pre-empt what it said was imminent Turkish deployment; Türkiye later sought Netanyahu's Interpol arrest on genocide and torture charges, and disputes also run through Somalia/Somaliland recognition, Eastern Mediterranean energy ties and a Türkiye-Saudi-Pakistan defence pact. The NYT reports US envoy Tom Barrack called the strike an "unnecessary escalation" as both NATO ally Türkiye and US-backed Israel pursue clashing visions for Syria after Gaza and Assad's 2024 ouster.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.6_

### 4. Turkey, Saudi, Pak to hold first defence pact meeting

Turkey will host Saudi Arabia and Pakistan on Monday for the first ministerial meeting of their new Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, which commits the three countries to mutual defence if any is attacked. The Istanbul meeting will set up a political and defence strategic committee of foreign and defence ministers and military chiefs. Pakistan is sending Deputy PM and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar with defence officials; Saudi Arabia is sending its foreign and defence ministers and chief of general staff. The pact unites NATO's second-largest army with the Islamic world's only nuclear-armed state and a leading Gulf power, and participants plan to address defence capabilities, force interoperability and defence industry cooperation.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.11; The Hindu Mangaluru p.16_

### 5. Death toll rises to 38 from Russia's attack on Ukraine

The death toll from a Russian strike on a warehouse west of Kyiv rose to 38 with four still missing, President Zelenskyy said Sunday — the war's deadliest attack so far this year. Friday night's strike on what Ukrainian officials called an ammunition storage facility in Myla sparked massive explosions and fires through nearby homes; 20 were injured including two children. Russia's Defence Ministry said Sunday it is preparing "massive strikes" against Ukraine's energy infrastructure in response to Kyiv's attacks on Russia's civilian infrastructure and fuel and energy sector.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangalore p.11_

## Top 5 — Sports

### 1. Struthoff's strike extends Germany's reign

Germany edged Spain 1-0 in a cagey men's Hockey World Cup final at the Belifus Arena in Wavre on Sunday to claim a second successive title, with Michel Struthoff rounding off keeper Luis Calzado late in the third quarter for the decisive goal. Spain, chasing a first crown, took off Calzado with less than four minutes left but could not break down a resolute German defence; head coach Andre Henning's side, with only nine returnees from the 2023 group, equalled a record fourth World Cup crown. In the third-place match, Argentina beat the Netherlands 2-1.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.17_

### 2. Robinson rocks as England wins series

England clinched a series victory over Pakistan with a thumping 194-run win in the second Test at Lord's on Sunday, as Pakistan, set an improbable target of 389, was dismissed for 194 on the fourth day to hand England an unbeatable 2-0 lead in the three-match series. Pacer Ollie Robinson tormented Pakistan again with two wickets in four balls before Jofra Archer dismissed captain Babar Azam, and while Saud Shakeel (97) restored some self-respect, Josh Tongue polished off the tail; earlier, 36-year-old Mohammad Abbas made it to the Lord's honours board for the first time after bowling England out for 208.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.20; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.12_

### 3. Shafali on song before bowlers choke Thailand

Shafali Verma smashed a 36-ball 64 with eight fours and three sixes as India raced to 100 in 10 overs before Thailand's spinners, led by Suleeporn Laomi (three for 26), triggered a collapse to restrict the seven-time champion to 159 for nine in its Asia Cup match in Dubai on Sunday. In reply, Thailand was bundled out for 65 in the 17th over, with only Nannapat Koncharoenkai (41) offering resistance; Nandani Sharma (three for 18), Shree Charani (two for 10), and Deepti Sharma (three for zero) shared the spoils. Shafali was named Player of the Match; India's next match is on September 3 against Hong Kong in Dubai at 8 p.m. IST.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.17; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.12_

### 4. Kaverappa's strikes put South in command on the opening day

Vidwath Kaverappa (four for 66) helped South Zone take the honours on day one of its Duleep Trophy semifinal against North Zone at the BCCI Centre of Excellence in Bengaluru on Sunday, as North was shot out for 195 after opting to bat, with opener Qamran Iqbal scoring 76 (182b, 10x4, 1x6) but reprieved twice by Ricky Bhui. At stumps, South was 40 for two after losing Bhui and Shaik Rasheed in the space of 16 balls, with much depending on how the batting line-up — sans Karun Nair, who was not included in the playing XI — tackles the North pace trio of Gurnoor Brar, Arshdeep Singh and Anshul Kamboj; on the adjacent ground, defending champion Central Zone made 266 against East Zone, with Rinku Singh top-scoring with 60 (88b, 8x4) and a 45-run last-wicket stand between Aryan Panday (22) and Yash Thakur (30 n.o.).
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.20; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.13_

### 5. Fame was never the goal, but this is one of the greatest honours: Federer

A tearful Roger Federer was inducted into the International Tennis Hall of Fame on Saturday in Newport, Rhode Island, calling formal entry into the sport's pantheon one of the "greatest honours of my life." The 20-time Grand Slam champion and 45-year-old Swiss, who retired in 2022, was frequently overcome with emotion as he paid tribute to opponents, officials, fans, family, and friends in a lengthy address. "They call this the Hall of Fame, but fame was never the goal," he said, also name-checking idol Stefan Edberg and joking he needed tissues and sunglasses as he wiped away tears, and adding that while his time as a player is done, he is still writing his love letter to tennis.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.17; Deccan Herald Mangalore p.12_
