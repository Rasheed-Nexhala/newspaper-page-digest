### Externment order can't be passed routinely, says Supreme Court

The Supreme Court held that an externment order directly impinges on fundamental freedoms and cannot be passed as a matter of routine, and that multiple criminal cases alone do not justify externment. A bench of Justices Dipankar Datta and Sheel Nagu said the District Magistrate must have bona fide subjective satisfaction with objective material and a discernible nexus before issuing such an order. The court quashed the November 4, 2025 externment order against Vijay Kumar Rajpoot alias Viju by the Raigarh District Magistrate and faulted the Chhattisgarh High Court for a hands-off approach, allowing him to return to the districts from which he had been externed.

### Indus water pact in force, rules Hague court; India rejects

A World Bank-constituted court of arbitration ruled on Monday that the more than six-decade-old Indus Water Treaty between India and Pakistan remains fully in force despite New Delhi putting it in abeyance after the April 22, 2025 Pahalgam terror attack that killed 26 Indian citizens. India rejected the Hague-based award as an illegally constituted body with no jurisdiction over sovereign decisions, while the tribunal unanimously found no grounds for suspension or termination and barred India from concreting the dam wall and power intake of the 850-MW Ralte Hydroelectric Plant on the Chenab beyond certain levels until 90 days after the Neutral Expert's final decision, expected in July 2027.

### SC: Real victim can settle for quashing criminal cases

The Supreme Court ruled that the real victim of a crime—not necessarily the informant or complainant—is authorised to enter into a settlement that can form the basis for quashing criminal proceedings under the High Court's Section 482 inherent power, while compounding under Section 320 CrPC (now Section 359 BNSS) remains strictly statutory. A bench of Justices K V Vishwanathan and Arun Palli drew the distinction while hearing an appeal by Anand Kumar alias Sanjay Lalwani against a Madhya Pradesh High Court order refusing to quash a 2018 criminal case because the informant was not party to the compromise.

### Over 6 crore names vanish from draft voters' lists in 17 states, UTs

Nearly 6.15 crore names have been deleted from draft electoral rolls in 17 states and Union Territories during the third phase of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR), pruning lists from 36.08 crore voters to 29.93 crore—a 17.04% (6,15,07,279) reduction owing to death, permanent shifting, multiple enrolments and other reasons. Delhi recorded the highest proportion of deletions at 32.78% (47.56 lakh), Maharashtra the highest number at 2.06 crore (21.15%), and Karnataka 1.07 crore names omitted (19.47%), with Telangana third at 73.39 lakh.

### In firepower boost, Tatas to make US Javelin missiles in India

Tata Advanced Systems on Monday signed a Memorandum of Understanding with Javelin Joint Venture of Lockheed Martin and Raytheon as the "prime partner" for commercial production of Javelin anti-tank guided missiles in India, days after India and the US inked a $45-million government-to-government contract to purchase 100 Javelin systems. The firms will explore a final assembly and integration facility in India; sub-assembly kits and guidance electronics will be made at Lockheed's Troy, Alabama, and Raytheon's Tucson, Arizona, facilities before shipment for final assembly.
