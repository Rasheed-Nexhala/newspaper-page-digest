Headline: 14 years on... still failing our daughters  
Section: MEMORIES OF NIRBHAYA  
Body: UP teen gang-raped in bus from Noida to Delhi

A 16-year-old who boarded a bus in Greater Noida on a rained out night earlier this month was allegedly gang-raped by the conductor and driver and abandoned in Kashmere Gate, more than 40 km away, police said. The two accused have been arrested.

The crime, recalling the 2012 Nirbhaya gang-rape and murder, took place on the night of August 4 but has come to light only now. Upset after being scolded by her mother, the girl from Mainpuri in Uttar Pradesh left home and got onto a bus for Noida Sector 63, where her cousin lives, police officials said.

It was raining and she mistakenly got off at Pari Chowk around 9 pm, the Class 11 student told police. Standing in pouring rain, she realised she was still about 25 km away from her cousin's home. While she was searching for transportation, a bus approached and stopped near her.

According to police, the conductor allegedly informed her that they were headed to Noida Sector 63 and would drop her off there. Trusting his words, she boarded the bus.

The girl was abandoned at Kashmere Gate, from where she managed to take an auto-rickshaw to the police station.

The accused, bus driver Kuldeep (39) and conductor Sandeep (26), were arrested from a parking lot.

Headline: Nepal flood toll 900; over 4,200 still missing  
Body: Kathmandu, Agencies: The toll in the devastating flash floods and mudslides caused by a glacial collapse in Nepal has gone over 900, with more than 4,200 people reported missing. The disaster, triggered by a wave of ice, rocks, and debris along the Bhote Koshi River, has affected central and northern regions, leaving at least 933 workers trapped across six hydropower projects. Prime Minister Balendra Shah described the situation as an "unimaginable and heartbreaking" calamity.

Among the missing are 590 foreign nationals and 99 security personnel. Rescue efforts face severe challenges due to adverse weather, rugged terrain, and flood damage. Authorities struggle to manage recovered bodies.

Headline: Sonia Gandhi memoir: Penguin cites 'particular issue'  
Body: Press Trust of India  
New Delhi

Maintaining that there was no external pressure behind its decision to not publish Sonia Gandhi's memoir, Penguin Random House India on Monday said there was an impasse over "a particular issue" during discussions on "Belonging: A Journey of Love".

The publisher didn't disclose the contentious issue, citing "agreement and author confidentiality".

The fresh clarification comes amid a controversy over the India publication of the Congress leader's much anticipated memoir following an announcement by the US-based Alfred A Knopf, an imprint of Penguin Random House, that the book would be released on November 10.
