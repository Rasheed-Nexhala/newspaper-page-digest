## Top 5 — Mangaluru

### 1. Khader inspects DC office premises ahead of Cabinet meeting in M'luru
Health and Family Welfare Minister U T Khader inspected the Dakshina Kannada Deputy Commissioner's office at Padil on Sunday ahead of a Cabinet meeting in Mangaluru on September 18, saying Chief Minister D K Shivakumar wanted it held at a government institution and that consultations with local MLAs and stakeholders would prepare coastal and Dakshina Kannada development proposals for Cabinet consideration. Khader defended the chief minister's aerial survey of drought-affected areas as giving a comprehensive picture of damage, urged the Opposition not to indulge in petty politics over disasters, and said the government had taken a clear stand on Nagendra's resignation on moral grounds.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 2. Mangaluru Railway Region transition: Mysuru, Palakkad officials to meet this week
Divisional Railway Managers of Mysuru (SWR) and Palakkad (SR) divisions will meet in Mangaluru on Thursday to finalise the transition of the Mangaluru Railway Region from Palakkad to Mysuru, effective October 1 per an August 21 Railway Ministry order. SWR jurisdiction will extend to Ullal (inclusive) from Padil and till Thokur (exclusive) north — about 30 km of the 39 km route to Mysuru — covering roughly 2,000 railwaymen; Mangaluru Central handles about 30 arrivals/departures and the Junction about 70 halting plus 10 originating trains, with Central housing a Palakkad coaching depot of about 17 pairs and ~450 coaches, and Panambur Yard handling NMPA freight.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.3_

### 3. Rs 30L special grant gets road from Jeppu Shrine towards Netravati, started
Road development from the vicinity of Jeppu Guligajja Daivasthana in Attavara Ward 55 of Mangaluru City Corporation to the Netravati river will proceed with a special grant of ₹30 lakh, after guddali puja under MLA Vedavyasa Kamath. Kamath said the main road touching the national highway and leading to Guligajja Katte, plus a drain, aim to end severe waterlogging during rains, following requests from local people and former corporation member Shailesh B. Shetty, with the Public Works Department taking up the work; he added that development grants are lower than under the previous BJP government but funds for this project are being arranged from the limited state Congress grant.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

### 4. MCC Commissioner inspects infra projects
Mangaluru City Corporation Commissioner Rahul Ratnam Pandey inspected the proposed multi-level car parking facility at the old bus stand on K S Rao Road under the PPP model, where MSCL General Manager Arun Prabha said around 84 landowners had not agreed to part with properties and the old bus stand is more than seven decades old with a proposal to lease land for 30 years. Pandey directed officials to submit a month-wise report for the next 15 months within a week, and at the Surathkal-Idya wet well he directed water samples be sent for laboratory testing after residents complained of groundwater contamination affecting local wells.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 5. MLA orders evening cop cover at Darbe Circle
Puttur Congress MLA Ashok Rai has ordered a police arrangement near Darbe circle from 3 p.m. to 4.30 p.m., when St. Philomena College, the high school, Bethany School and Little Flower School let out and students scramble for buses amid moving traffic. After visiting the circle on Saturday evening and hearing from rickshaw drivers, shop owners and parents, Rai said personnel will be posted for children's safety and directed the municipality to shift the auto stand backward, demolish an old building there, and make provision for small stall-shops on the site.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.3_

## Top 5 — Coastal Karnataka

### 1. KSRTC bus catches fire
A KSRTC Sarige bus travelling from BC Road to Bengaluru via Subrahmanya was gutted after catching fire near Kaikamba on the Kaikamba-Gundya road in Bilinele late on Saturday night, but all 30-35 passengers were evacuated safely after the BC Road depot crew noticed the fire and alerted them. KSRTC officials from the Puttur division inspected the spot and initiated an inquiry as the exact cause is yet to be established.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 2. 'Lone jumbo being closely monitored'
Forest Department officials are closely monitoring a lone elephant spotted near SDM Engineering College campus at Ujire on Sunday morning after it moved from the Uppinangady side towards Kalmanja and Dharmasthala. Belthangady Range Forest Officer Mahesh Devadiga said Elephant Task Force personnel and forest staff fired warning shots into the air to drive the animal away from human settlements, and the elephant has started moving towards the forest area as surveillance continues.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

### 3. Facilitation centres to boost tourism in coastal region
Karnataka's Tourism Department plans single-window facilitation centres to help investors and boost coastal tourism through wellness, cruise and boathouse tourism inspired by Kerala, including medium-scale affordable spas and boathouse projects pending KTCDA and Coastal Business Zone approvals. Tourism Secretary Ram Prasath Manohar said coastal areas are untouched opportunities where cruises from Mumbai to Lakshadweep via Mangaluru could be encouraged to keep passengers overnight, and a travel circuit mixing water sports is being created.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.6_

### 4. First training workshop for Kambala jockeys held
Former Minister V. Sunil Kumar inaugurated the first Kambala jockeys training workshop at the Lava-Kusha Jodukare Kambala venue in Miyar, Karkala, on Sunday, jointly organised by the Karnataka State Kambala Association and District Kambala Committee. The workshop drew 214 candidates from Dakshina Kannada, Udupi and Kasaragod.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.3_

### 5. MAHE signs MoU with University of Sydney
Manipal Academy of Higher Education and the University of Sydney signed a Memorandum of Understanding at the Australian Consulate-General Residence in Mumbai to strengthen collaboration in education, research, innovation and international mobility, covering flagship research programmes, joint research funding and doctoral education, student and faculty mobility, innovation and knowledge translation, and joint governance with measurable outcomes. MAHE Vice Chancellor Dr Sharath K Rao and University of Sydney Deputy Vice Chancellor (Research) Prof Mike Ryan signed the agreement in the presence of Australian High Commissioner Philip Green OAM and Consul-General Paul Murphy.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.3_

## Top 5 — Karnataka

### 1. State files plea before CWMA seeking cut in water release
Karnataka has appealed to the CWMA to reduce releases to Tamil Nadu from 9,000 to 2,000 cusecs ahead of CWRC/CWMA meetings on September 8, after an August 25 order totalling up to 11.66 tmcft. Inflows are 49.18% below the 30-year average.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.4_

### 2. K'taka to open govt doors for startups
Startups in Karnataka may soon participate in state procurement and receive government work orders and support of up to Rs 25 lakh, following IT-BT Minister Priyank Kharge's announcement that the government will be the first customer of startups working on governance and public service delivery. The IT-BT department has drawn up specifics of the 'Government First' initiative—earmarking Rs 25 crore to support 100 startups over three years—and will place the proposal before the Cabinet, relaxing barriers such as prior experience, earnest money deposits and performance security while enabling departments to share datasets, space and problem statements with startups.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.1; Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.7_

### 3. Depleting water in dams: Agri scenario grim in Mysuru region
Declining water in Kabini and KRS reservoirs after releases to Tamil Nadu has left storage sufficient only for drinking water, with Mysuru district achieving just 44.11% of its 3,76,609-hectare sowing target (1,66,441 hectares) and the region expected to record less than 50% sowing overall. District in-charge Minister Yathindra Siddaramaiah has restricted releases to filling lakes and ponds, not paddy; Mandya farmers were told not to cultivate paddy while Hassan farmers were urged to grow ragi on 93,004 of 2,86,186 targeted hectares amid deficit rains.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.6_

### 4. Siddaramaiah demands 75% reservation for Dalits, BCs, minorities
Former Karnataka Chief Minister Siddaramaiah on Sunday called for implementation of the caste census and increase of reservation for Dalits, backward communities and minorities to 75 per cent at the Karnataka State Backward Castes Federation's Silver Jubilee celebration in Bengaluru. He said the caste survey report submitted by the Backward Classes Commission headed by Madhusudan Naik, which he had accepted before demitting office, must be implemented but a court order currently prevents it from being implemented or made public. Siddaramaiah said reservation according to caste and population is necessary in the Lok Sabha and Legislative Assembly to achieve political equality, and highlighted that the Backward Classes Department's budget had increased from Rs 5 crore when established to Rs 4,461 crore in 2026-27, with 2,712 hostels housing 2.58 lakh backward-community students.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.1; News Trail Mangaluru p.5; The Hindu Mangaluru p.4_

### 5. Prisoner videos trigger action: Kalaburagi jail chief SP suspended
Dr. Anita R., Chief Superintendent of Kalaburagi Central Prison, has been suspended following allegations of dereliction of duty in connection with videos titled "Prisoners' Bindass Life" showing inmate activities circulated on social and visual media platforms. Director General of Police, Prisons and Correctional Services, Alok Kumar issued the order in accordance with a government order dated September 3, with a departmental inquiry ordered and suspension effective immediately; during the suspension period her headquarters have been shifted to the New Central Prison in Vijayapura, where she must report and may not leave without prior permission. She was relieved of responsibility at the Prison Staff Training Institute in Mysuru until further government orders.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.5_

## Top 5 — India

### 1. 5 die as boys' PG collapses in Delhi
An around 50-year-old five-storey building housing a boys' hostel in Satya Niketan near Delhi University's South Campus collapsed on Sunday afternoon, leaving five students dead and many trapped, with locals claiming 30-50 people may have been inside. Two people pulled from the debris were brought dead to hospital and three died during treatment; four were being treated at AIIMS Trauma Centre, while repair work was underway in the basement PG accommodation and two NDRF teams comprising 70 personnel and a canine squad were deployed after locals said rainwater collected in the basement had weakened the foundation.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.1; Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.7; The Hindu Mangaluru p.1; The Hindu Mangaluru p.8_

### 2. Over 22 lakh affected by floods in Bihar
More than 22 lakh people in 12 districts of Bihar have been affected by a deluge as rivers overflowed following heavy rain in the state and neighbouring Jharkhand, with the Ganga crossing its highest flood level in Patna on Sunday at 50.57 metres—0.05 metres above the previous HFL of 50.52 metres recorded on August 21. East Central Railway cancelled and diverted some trains because of alarmingly high water at a bridge connecting Punpun and Parsa Bazar stations; 190 community kitchens and 1,429 boats are in service, 27 SDRF and 10 NDRF teams are deployed, and around 22.42 lakh people in Patna, Bhojpur, Saran, Vaishali, Bhagalpur, Begusarai, Buxar, Samastipur, Munger, Katihar, Lakhisarai and Arwal are affected, with September rainfall so far at 69.1 mm, 55% above normal.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.2; News Trail Mangaluru p.7_

### 3. 10 dead after consuming spurious liquor in MP's Sagar
Ten persons died and others fell ill after consuming spurious liquor in two villages of Banda tehsil in Madhya Pradesh's Sagar district on Sunday, though local BJP MLA Virendra Lodhi said 11 had died and around 25 were shifted from Banda Civil Hospital for advanced care. Chief Minister Mohan Yadav ordered a probe and strict action, the administration sealed seven liquor shops as a precaution, and senior officials including the collector and superintendent of police reached the affected area; the tragedy follows 13 deaths from spurious liquor in Gujarat's Bhavnagar last month.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.11; News Trail Mangaluru p.7; The Hindu Mangaluru p.1; The Hindu Mangaluru p.8_

### 4. No 'space' for job loss, say ISRO staff amid privatisation moves
Two days after the Indian Space Research Organisation's Staff Association wrote to chairperson V Narayanan expressing concerns over the organisation's privatisation plans, members clarified they are not against private players entering the space industry but want ISRO to clearly communicate its future direction to employees. On 6 September an office bearer told The Print that if the government outsources tasks such as rocket building and technology development to private companies, staff need clarity on ISRO's role; on 4 September the association sent a five-page letter citing IN-SPACe chairperson Pawan Goenka's comments that ISRO will no longer make or manufacture launch vehicles, work that will go to the private sector or PSUs. With no official communication on transferring launch vehicle design and manufacturing, a VSSC office bearer said at least 50 per cent of the workforce could lose jobs if the private sector takes over, though employees are not completely hostile to privatisation if integration preserves the relevance of existing staff and centres.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.7; News Trail Mangaluru p.1; The Hindu Mangaluru p.10_

### 5. India, China hold talks in Arunachal Pradesh
Following the Galwan clashes, India and China continued military and diplomatic dialogue; on August 26 National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and China's Wang Yi held the 25th Special Representatives talks in Beijing and signed an eight-point outcome including two additional Border Personnel Meeting Points and new hotlines, with one new BPM expected in the Eastern Sector under 3 Corps and Army Chief General Dhiraj Seth recently reviewing operational preparedness along the frontier.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.1; The Hindu Mangaluru p.8_

## Top 5 — International

### 1. Indonesia cancels flights as volcano erupts
Indonesia's Anak Krakatau volcano erupted early Sunday for 30 seconds at 3:53 a.m., prompting cancellation of all flights at Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta International Airport as volcanic ash drifted across western Indonesia. Indonesia's Geological Agency said the Sunda Strait island had shown activity since Friday; no casualties or evacuation order was issued, though residents and visitors were warned to stay at least 3 kilometres from the active crater, with the nearest settlement more than 16 kilometres away.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.14; News Trail Mangaluru p.9; The Hindu Mangaluru p.17_

### 2. Over 140 killed in Yemen clashes
Fighting between Yemeni government forces and Iran-backed Houthi rebels in southwest Yemen killed more than 140 combatants between midday Saturday and early Sunday, military sources said. Two Saudi-backed government officials told AFP that 84 of their troops were killed, mostly in missile attacks, while four Houthi military sources said 57 rebel fighters were killed in the same period.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.14; The Hindu Mangaluru p.12_

### 3. Iran strikes US ship in Strait of Hormuz
Iran said Sunday it struck an unmanned US vessel trying to enter the Strait of Hormuz, the latest salvo in a flare-up of fighting that began with US and Israeli attacks on Feb 28 and has persisted despite a June ceasefire announcement. The US did not immediately confirm the attack, which came a day after US forces struck three Iranian oil tankers in response to a missile attack on Navy warships; at least five people were killed earlier in the week during a US bombardment of southern Iran, and the Trump administration is responding militarily while also targeting foreign financial institutions handling Iran's money.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.9; The Hindu Mangaluru p.12_

### 4. Homes lost, hungry babies: Nepal's mothers struggle in shelters
Nepal's August 26 flash floods separated Sharmila Shrestha from her 14-month-old daughter for two or three days and destroyed her home; reunited at a crowded Kathmandu shelter, she can only feed her toddler the same heavy adult food as other evacuees because no baby food is available. The UNFPA estimates around 4,430 pregnant women are living across 15 municipalities in three flood-affected districts based on an affected population of about 310,000, with roughly 492 births expected in the following month and about 74 women potentially requiring emergency obstetric care. Mothers report struggling to breastfeed, sleeping on classroom floors, and worrying about reaching hospitals if complications arise.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.14_

### 5. US envoys arrive in Kyiv after talks with Putin
US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held talks in Kyiv on Sunday in their first official visit to Ukraine as part of a renewed push for peace more than 4½ years after Russia's full-scale invasion. The trip follows their Saturday meeting with Vladimir Putin in Moscow as both sides escalate aerial attacks; President Zelenskyy said Ukraine is ready for a "constructive" discussion and is interested in bringing the end of the war closer with needed security guarantees.
_Sources: News Trail Mangaluru p.9_

## Top 5 — Sports

### 1. Sat-Chi master China
Star Indian men's doubles pair Satwiksairaj Rankireddy and Chirag Shetty rallied from a set down to beat China's He Ji Ting and Ren Xiang Yu 11-21, 21-13, 21-17 and clinch the China Masters Super 750 title in Shenzhen on Sunday, their 10th BWF World Tour victory and second title of 2026 after the Singapore Open. The world No. 5 pair, playing their third final at the tournament after runner-up finishes in 2023 and 2025, said the win gives them a huge boost ahead of defending their Asian Games gold in Japan from September 19.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.15; News Trail Mangaluru p.12; The Hindu Mangaluru p.13_

### 2. India end wait of two decades
India qualified for the AFC U-20 Asian Cup main tournament for the first time in 20 years after a commanding 3-0 win over Bangladesh in their final group match in Tashkent on Sunday. The win took India to six points from three matches, and with Syria losing 0-3 to Uzbekistan later on Sunday, India finished second in Group B as one of the seven best runners-up teams across eight qualifying groups.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.15_

### 3. Antonelli dazzles to Italian GP victory
Kimi Antonelli won the Italian Grand Prix at Monza on Sunday with a charge from 19th on the grid—after exceeding permitted power-unit changes—to beat Mercedes teammate George Russell by 3.857 seconds, with Max Verstappen third, Lando Norris fourth, Oscar Piastri fifth and Lewis Hamilton sixth after Charles Leclerc crashed out triggering an early red flag. Antonelli's seventh win of the season and first home GP victory stretched his drivers' championship lead to 66 points over Russell (267 to 201), with Mercedes leading the constructors' standings on 468 points.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.16; The Hindu Mangaluru p.12_

### 4. Duleep Trophy final: Kishan ton leads East's run feast vs South
Skipper Ishan Kishan struck an unbeaten 111 (14 fours, 1 six) as East Zone reached 385 for four against South Zone on the opening day of the Duleep Trophy final in Chennai on Sunday, with Kumar Kushagra 63 not out and a fifth-wicket stand of 144 in 26.4 overs. Other contributors were Vaibhav Sooryavanshi 44, Abhimanyu Easwaran 72 and Shikhar Mohan 85; Kishan completed his 10th first-class hundred and 4,000 first-class runs, while Mohammad Siraj went 14-1-96-0 as South struggled on a true pitch.
_Sources: Deccan Herald Mangaluru p.16; News Trail Mangaluru p.12_

### 5. 18 centuries: tracing Smriti Mandhana's journey to the top
Smriti Mandhana's 18th international century—a 124 off 57 balls against Hong Kong at the Women's T20 Asia Cup in Dubai on September 3—made her the highest run-scorer in women's international cricket with 10,880 runs, surpassing Mithali Raj's 10,868, and also broke the record for most centuries (18), passing Meg Lanning and Laura Wolvaardt; ten of her 18 hundreds have come in the last two years at a higher strike rate.
_Sources: The Hindu Mangaluru p.7_
